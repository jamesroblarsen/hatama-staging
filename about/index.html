About Hatama

Hatama is a free word game made by James Larsen. Guess a hidden five-letter word in Batak Toba, the language spoken around Lake Toba in North Sumatra, then see what it means. There is one word a day, the same for everyone, with a new one at midnight.

The name joins hata, “word”, with ma, a light Toba particle of emphasis.

Hatama is part of Glossle, a shelf of word puzzles in the world's other languages.

How it works

You have five guesses. After each one the tiles turn red for a letter in the right place, gold for a letter that is in the word somewhere else, and grey for a letter that is not in it. Answers use only the 18 letters of Toba spelling. The faded keys (c, f, v, w, y, z) are letters found only in borrowed words: you can type them, but they are never in the answer.

When the round ends you see the word in Latin letters and in surat Batak, the Toba script, with its meaning in English and Indonesian. More on the How to play page.

Where the words come from

Every answer appears in at least two independent Batak Toba dictionaries, and was then checked by a speaker. Words a speaker marked wrong never come up as answers, though you can still type them as guesses.
